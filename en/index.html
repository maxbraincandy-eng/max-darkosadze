I am interested in the space where scientific knowledge meets human experience.

An operating table, a cockpit, a lecture hall, a page — worlds that look far apart and are held together by one question: how do we decide when the information is never complete?

This site is an archive. It gathers what I do, read, write and think about — not because any of it is finished, but because it continues.

Discipline 01

Surgery

An operating room forgives nothing careless — which is exactly why it teaches precision everywhere else.

What matters most in surgery is not what happens at the table but what was settled before it: a plan that already contains what to do if something goes wrong.

At the table

An operation begins with a plan rather than an incision: the order of the steps, the fallback, and the minute where you stop and look again.

Deciding

The information is never complete. The skill that matters is knowing which step can wait and which one cannot.

The team

The most dangerous words in an operating room are “I assumed you knew”. Which is why the obvious is said out loud too.

Philosophy of medicine

A patient is not a case. A patient is a person with a story, whose trust is spent again at every visit.

Discipline 02

Aviation

There is a particular kind of silence above the clouds.

A flight is preceded by a briefing that often takes longer than the flight itself. Aviation teaches that confidence is born of preparation, never the other way round.

The briefing

Before the flight: weather, weight, fuel, the alternate — and the question a student remembers for a career: what will you do if something goes wrong, and at what moment will you decide it?

Instructing

An instructor's work is to leave the student able to think when the right-hand seat is empty. So you ask rather than answer.

Philosophy of flight

The checklist exists not because the pilot is not trusted, but because memory never is. Discipline here is not the opposite of freedom; it is its condition.

Discipline 03

Philosophy

To understand a life, one must first understand the ideas that built it.

Philosophy here is not an academic title. It is a set of questions that follow the day's work around: will, freedom, consciousness, meaning.

The reading

Nietzsche, Schopenhauer, Plato, Hegel — not as authorities but as people who asked the same questions first.

The questions

What obligation does freedom leave behind; where will ends and habit begins; how meaning differs from explanation.

Where it shows

In the operating room and the cockpit — wherever a decision has to be made without the full answer. Philosophy is the preparation for that minute.
